# Two Loose Ends: the 30-Day Notice Claim, and Clause 17(d)

---

## §1 — You told them you can buy out for 30 days. You can't. Fix it now.

### 1.1 What you said, and what the contract says

**You told the interviewer:** 60 days' notice, but you think you can buy out for 30.

**The contract (clause 12(d)) says:** you may give 60 days' notice **or gross salary for the mentioned duration, subject to the approval of the company.**

- *"The mentioned duration"* = the full **60 days**. There is no 30-day option anywhere in either document.
- The buy-out also requires **their approval** — it is not yours to elect.

So what you described is **not accurate**, and it will surface at exactly the worst moment: when ICICI tries to set a joining date and you have to explain why it is 60 days and not 30.

### 1.2 Why correcting it now is better than waiting

| If you correct it now | If it surfaces later |
|---|---|
| You look careful and organised | It looks like the date slipped, or like you overstated your availability |
| ICICI plans for 60 days from the start | ICICI builds a start date around 30 and has to re-plan — possibly against their project timeline |
| Small, forgettable correction | A credibility question at offer stage, in a negotiation where you are already asking for more money |

**A correction made early is a detail. The same correction made late is a pattern.** You have already had two rounds where something went unsaid; do not add a third by letting this one drift.

### 1.3 What to send — short, factual, no apology spiral

Send it to the recruiter or HR, by email, before the HR round or alongside it:

```
Hi [name],

A quick correction from the technical round, so you can plan accurately.

I mentioned a 60-day notice period and thought I might buy out part of it.
I've now re-read my contract: the notice period is 60 calendar days, and the
buy-out option covers the full 60 days — it isn't a 30-day option. It also
requires my employer's approval rather than being mine to elect.

So the safe planning assumption is 60 days from resignation. If my employer
agrees to release me earlier I'll let you know immediately, but I'd rather you
plan on 60 than be surprised later.

Happy to discuss on the HR call.

Best,
Prashanth
```

**Why this works:**
- **You frame it as accuracy for their benefit** — *"so you can plan accurately"* — not as a confession.
- **You correct the fact and the mechanism** in two sentences, so there is nothing left to clarify.
- **You offer the upside** — *"if they release me earlier I'll tell you"* — which is true, since clause 12(e) lets them.
- **You do not apologise three times.** One clean correction reads as professional. Repeated apology reads as a problem.

### 1.4 How to handle it if they push

**"But you said 30 days in the interview."**
> *"I did, and I was wrong — I hadn't re-read the buy-out clause. It covers the full 60 days and needs my employer's approval. I corrected it as soon as I checked, because I'd rather you plan on 60 than build a timeline on something I got wrong."*

**Do not** say *"I think I can still negotiate 30"* unless Black Piano has actually told you so in writing. If you have asked them and they have agreed, **that changes everything** — say so with the email in hand.

## §2 — Clause 17(d): what it actually requires, and when

**Read the clause precisely, because it has two obligations with different triggers:**

> *"If you receive an offer of employment, engagement or solicitation with a Person other than us, **either during your employment or during the period for which the restrictions set out above remain in force**, you will **immediately provide that Person with a complete copy of this clause and relevant definitions**; and **if you accept the offer, you will immediately notify us of the identity** of the [new employer]."*

### 2.1 Breaking it into two duties

| Duty | Who receives it | When it triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **Give a copy of the clause** | **ICICI** (the new employer) | On **receiving** an offer from them — *"immediately"* |
| **Notify the identity of the new employer** | **Black Piano** | On **accepting** the offer — *"immediately"* |

**Note the distinction:** the trigger for the ICICI disclosure is *receiving an offer*, not accepting it. The trigger for notifying Black Piano is *acceptance*.

### 2.2 What you must give ICICI — and what you must not

**You must give them a copy of *"this clause and relevant definitions."*** That means:

- **Contract clause 17** — "Restrictions After Employment", including the definitions in 17(a): *Client*, *Prospective Client*, *Person*, *Termination Date*.
- **That is all the clause requires.**

**It does NOT require:**
- Your full employment contract
- Your **NDA** — even though the NDA contains the more restrictive 24-month non-compete
- Your salary, payslips, or offer letter

**You are not obliged to show them your offer letter.** Nothing in clause 17(d) asks for it, and you should not volunteer it — your compensation is expressly confidential under both the offer letter and the increment letter.

### 2.3 But here is the judgement call you should make

**The clause requires the contract clause. The honest answer requires the NDA too.**

Clause 17(d) makes you disclose clause 17 — a **12-month non-solicitation**. It does not make you disclose the NDA's **24-month non-compete**. But you know about it, ICICI will be relying on your assurance that you are free to join, and if it emerges later you are the one who looks like they withheld something material.

**Disclose both. Volunteer the NDA clause 6.** It costs you nothing — ICICI is not a Black Piano client, so the clause does not bite — and it buys you three things:

1. **You satisfy the spirit of the clause, not just its letter.**
2. **You look diligent.** A candidate who discloses a restrictive covenant and explains why it doesn't apply reads as low-risk.
3. **You protect yourself.** If ICICI's legal team later objects, you want that objection *now*, while you still have a job — not at joining stage when you have already resigned.

**The framing:**

> *"My contract requires me to give you a copy of the post-employment restrictions, so here they are. There are two: a 12-month non-solicitation in the employment contract, and a 24-month non-compete in the NDA that restricts joining my employer's clients. I've checked, and ICICI Lombard is not a Black Piano client, so it doesn't apply to this move. I'm disclosing both anyway because I'd rather you have the full picture — and I'm getting the non-compete reviewed by a lawyer so there are no surprises."*

That paragraph makes you look like someone who reads contracts. Which, given the last few days, you now are.

### 2.4 When to do it — and when not to

| Timing | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Now / HR round** | Nothing yet. The clause triggers on *receiving an offer*, and you have not received one. |
| **When ICICI issues a written offer** | Send the clause copy to HR/recruiter with the framing above. This is the contractually correct moment. |
| **When you accept** | Notify Black Piano of the new employer's identity — *"immediately"* per the clause. |
| **Never** | Do not send your full contract, NDA, or payslips to satisfy a clause that only asks for one clause. Do not disclose anything to Black Piano before you have accepted a written ICICI offer. |

### 2.5 The Black Piano notification — practical note

**Black Piano will learn where you are going anyway**, through the resignation process and quite possibly through LinkedIn. So the notification is not a secret you are keeping — it is a formality the contract requires.

**Keep it flat and factual when you resign:**

> *"I'm resigning with 60 days' notice. My new employer is ICICI Lombard, as required under clause 17(d) of my contract."*

No explanation, no justification, no mention of the buy-out request in the same breath. If you are going to ask them to approve a buy-out, **do it in a separate conversation after the resignation is acknowledged** — combining *"I'm leaving"* with *"and please release me early"* gives them one decision to make instead of two, and it will be a no.

## §3 — Revised sequence

1. **Today:** send the 30-day correction (§1.3). Accuracy first.
2. **Today/tomorrow:** ask Black Piano **in writing** whether they will approve a buy-out, and on what terms. Do not resign.
3. **When ICICI issues a written offer:** send them the clause copy — contract clause 17 **and** the NDA clause 6, with the framing in §2.3.
4. **Get the NDA clause 6 reviewed by a lawyer** before you accept. ICICI not being a client makes this low-risk, but "low-risk" is not "no-risk" and the consultation is cheap.
5. **When you accept:** resign with 60 days' notice, notify Black Piano of the new employer, and *then* raise the buy-out as a separate request.
6. **Never resign before a written ICICI offer is in hand.**
