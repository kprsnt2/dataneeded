§1 — The notice period, clause by clause

1.1 The three sources, quoted

Employment Contract, clause 12(d) — you resigning:

"You may terminate your employment with the Company, without any cause, by giving not less than 60 (sixty) calendar days (as per the clauses mentioned below) prior notice or gross salary for the mentioned duration, subject to the approval of the company."

60 calendar days. Confirmed. No ambiguity.

1.2 The buy-out — what the contract actually permits

You can offer to pay. You cannot insist on paying. Clause 12(d) attaches two conditions to the buy-out route:

  1. "subject to the approval of the company" — the buy-out is a request, not a right.
  2. "or gross salary for the mentioned duration" — the mentioned duration is the full 60 days. There is no clause anywhere that lets you pay for 30 days.

So the accurate statement is:

"I can request to buy out the full 60-day notice by paying gross salary in lieu. Whether I can leave earlier is entirely at Black Piano's discretion."

1.3 And there is a second lever you should know about — clause 12(e)

"It will be at the discretion of the company to relieve you prior to the completion of the notice period and is non-negotiable."

Read that carefully, because it cuts against you in a negotiation:

  • The company may release you early — at its sole discretion.
  • That discretion is "non-negotiable" — you cannot demand early release, and you cannot challenge a refusal.

The practical consequence: if Black Piano decides to hold you for the full 60 days, you have no contractual route out other than their goodwill. If they decide to release you in two weeks, you have no right to stay and be paid for 60. The discretion is entirely theirs.

§3 — ⚠️ The NDA's 24-month non-compete — read this before accepting anything

This is the most consequential clause in either document, and it is more restrictive than the employment contract.

3.1 What clause 6 says, verbatim

"Employee / Consultant / Intern / Others inclusive of his / her direct beneficiaries in business, interest and title in recognition of the transfer of Confidential and Proprietary Information to Black Piano and / or its clients and customers hereby agrees not to directly or indirectly join the clients and / or compete with the business of Black Piano and / or its clients and customers and its successors and assigns during the term of the agreement and for a period of 2 years [Two Years] following the expiration or termination of this contract and notwithstanding the cause or reason for termination."

3.3 The key phrase: "join the clients"

*The NDA does not merely stop you soliciting clients. It stops you joining them.* That is a direct restriction on your next employer, not on your conduct.

The question that decides everything: is ICICI Lombard a client of Black Piano?

  • Black Piano serves US and UK clients across industry sectors.
  • ICICI Lombard is an Indian insurer.
  • On what I can see, they are not the same — and if ICICI is not a Black Piano client, clause 6's "join the clients" limb does not reach them.
  • But "compete with the business of Black Piano and/or its clients and customers" is broader, and the 24-month lookback for "Prospective Client" widens it further.

You know who your clients are. I don't. Ask yourself plainly: has Black Piano ever served ICICI Lombard, or a company I would be competing against by doing this job? If the answer is no, the clause most likely does not bite. If you are unsure, get a lawyer to look at it — this is exactly the situation where a one-hour consultation is worth more than the fee.

3.4 The honest legal position in India

Post-employment non-compete clauses are generally unenforceable in India. Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 voids agreements in restraint of trade, and Indian courts have consistently declined to enforce clauses that stop a former employee from taking up employment elsewhere. Non-solicitation and confidentiality clauses are treated differently — those are generally upheld, because they protect a legitimate proprietary interest rather than restraining trade.

So the 24-month non-compete is very likely unenforceable as written. But:

  • That is a court's conclusion, not a clause you can rely on in advance.
  • Unenforceable does not mean costless. Black Piano could send a legal notice, withhold your final settlement, or make the next few months unpleasant. Defending a claim costs time and money even when you win.
  • The NDA names the District and Session Court, Pune as the jurisdiction, and includes an express injunction clause — meaning they have written in the remedy of seeking a restraining order.

I am not your lawyer and this is not advice. Get a professional opinion on clause 6 specifically, before you resign.

3.5 A clause you may not have noticed — and it obliges you

Employment Contract, clause 17(d):

"If you receive an offer of employment… with a Person other than us, either during your employment or during the period for which the restrictions set out above remain in force, you will immediately provide that Person with a complete copy of this clause and relevant definitions; and if you accept the offer, you will immediately notify us of the identity…"

You are contractually required to give ICICI a copy of the restrictive-covenant clause, and to tell Black Piano who your new employer is.

What this means practically:

  • Do not sign anything at ICICI that warrants you have no restrictive covenants. If they ask, disclose — the clause requires it, and hiding it is worse if it surfaces later.
  • Do not resign before you have told ICICI. If ICICI's own compliance or legal team has an issue with the non-compete, you want to know that before you are unemployed.
  • Expect ICICI to ask about notice period and covenants. Have the clause ready and describe it accurately — "24-month non-compete in the NDA; I'm advised post-employment non-competes are generally unenforceable in India, but I'm disclosing it in full."

§4 — What to do, in order

Before you resign:

  1. Get a lawyer to read NDA clause 6 and contract clause 17 against the specific facts — who Black Piano's clients are, and whether ICICI or its group overlaps. One consultation. This is the single highest-value action available to you.
  2. Ask Black Piano, in writing, whether they will approve a buy-out and on what terms. Email, not a phone call. You want the answer in writing, and you want to know whether they will waive or narrow the restrictions.
  3. Do not resign until ICICI has issued a written offer. Never resign against a verbal promise.
  4. Tell ICICI the truth about the 60 days and the restrictions. The contract obliges you to disclose the covenant anyway, and a new employer that discovers a concealed non-compete at joining stage can withdraw the offer.
  5. Confirm your actual monthly gross from your payslip before discussing buy-out numbers.

In the ICICI negotiation:

  1. Your notice period is a live constraint. ICICI will ask how soon you can join. The honest answer is: "60 days contractually; I can request a buy-out but it needs my employer's approval, so I'd plan for 60."
