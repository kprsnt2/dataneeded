So ₹30 L is roughly ₹78,000/month more in hand than today — and after Mumbai rent and higher living costs, you are still ₹16,000–35,000/month ahead, while living in a city you did not choose with costs you did not have.

That is a real gain rather than a wash. And the gap between ₹25 L and ₹30 L is not 20% in your pocket — it is roughly ₹33,000/month, because of how the tax slabs fall. Every rupee of base above ₹25 L is worth more to you than the equivalent rupee below it.

My read: at ₹30 L, take it — subject to the four checks in §0C.

0C — The four things to verify before you say yes at ₹30 L

  1. The fixed/variable split. If ₹30 L includes a large performance bonus, your monthly take-home is materially lower and this maths weakens. Ask what is fixed.
  2. Permanence and funding. You would be relocating for a pilot the recruiter himself said "might not work." At ₹30 L the money is good — but if the programme is paused in eight months you have taken on rent, a move and a job hunt. Ask whether the role is permanent and funded beyond phase one.
  3. What success looks like at six months. A high offer raises expectations. On a pilot, "success" can be undefined — and an undefined bar you are paid well to clear is a trap. Get it named.
  4. Relocation support. Still worth asking for even at ₹30 L. It is a different budget line and often easier to grant than base.

0D — What you are still trading away

Money is not the only axis, and at ₹30 L it is easy to stop looking:

  • Your own house — no rent, no landlord, no deposit
  • Remote work — full flexibility, no commute
  • Hyderabad — your city, your network, lower cost of living
  • Time — a Mumbai commute is hours a week you do not currently spend

The honest framing: ₹30 L buys you a materially better income and costs you a way of living. Whether that trade is right depends on how much you value the second list — which is your call, not arithmetic.

But note what it does for your career: ₹30 L resets your market value permanently. Your next role benchmarks against ₹30 L, not ₹12.7 L. Over a career, that compounds far beyond a year's rent.

§5 — The case for taking it anyway

I have given you the financial case against. Here is the honest case for, because it is real and you should weigh it properly:

  1. ₹25 LPA resets your market value. Your next role will be benchmarked against ₹25 L, not ₹12.7 L. Over a career that compounds far more than one year's rent.
  2. It is a genuine agentic AI role. You have spent three years building this capability on your own time. This is the first paid role that uses it.
  3. The title and department matter. "Agentic AI" at a listed insurer is a stronger line than "Data Analyst" for everything that follows.
  4. You are 12+ years in. Another year at ₹12.7 L in a Data Analyst title is a year of the same, and the market prices you on your last title.
  5. Hyderabad is not going anywhere. If it doesn't work, you can return — with a materially higher number on your CV.

The reframe that matters: this is not a salary decision, it is a career-optionality decision with a one-to-two year cost. If the role genuinely builds agentic AI experience at an insurer, the rent is the price of admission. If it turns out to be claims operations with an AI label — which §3 of the HR prep flags as a real risk — then you are paying a lot for very little.

So the question to answer is not "is ₹25 L enough?" It is "is this role what it says it is?" That is what to probe in the HR round.

§6 — What to ask before you answer

  1. "What's the fixed/variable split of the ₹25 L?" — if variable is large, the monthly number is smaller than it looks.
  2. "What relocation support is available?" — the single highest-value ask.
  3. "Is there a joining bonus?" — off-band, easier to approve.
  4. "Can the role be hybrid?" — changes the rent maths materially.
  5. "Is the position permanent, and is the programme funded beyond phase one?" — a role on an experiment that ends takes the relocation money with it.
  6. "What is the team, and is there an engineering function, or am I the hands-on developer?" — determines whether the career case in §5 is real.
  7. "What does success look like at six months?" — if nobody can say, the career case is weak.

  • Your actual take-home with a CA, not my estimate

Never:

  • Decide on money alone if the role is genuinely agentic AI
  • Decide on the role's promise alone if it turns out to be claims operations with an AI label
